Data Sources

Every number in IDP is traceable. Here are the primary datasets, standards, and references behind the predictions.

👥 Population Data

UN World Urbanization Prospects (WUP) 2024

Primary source for the dense 5-year interval dataset (1980–2025). Uses "urban agglomeration" boundaries — the entire continuous metro area — for planning accuracy. Published by the UN DESA Population Division.

Primary
Census of India (2001, 2011, 2021)

Used to ground and verify historical data points. Conducted decennially by the Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, Government of India.

Verified By
Worldometers — Largest Cities in India

Used to cross-validate 2025 population estimates for all 15 cities and to calibrate the live-ticker simulation on the homepage.

🤖 AI Prediction Models

Five models compete for each prediction. The one with the highest cross-validated R² score wins and makes the final forecast.

Logistic Growth (Verhulst S-Curve)
Gold Standard

Models natural population plateaus. Uses scipy.optimize.curve_fit to estimate a carrying capacity K — the city's maximum sustainable population given land area constraints.

Ridge Polynomial Regression (Degree 3)

Regularized polynomial using L2 penalty (alpha=1.0) to prevent wild extrapolation at longer time horizons. Best for cities with complex growth trajectories (rapid acceleration followed by slowdown).

Polynomial Regression (Degree 2)

Captures simple acceleration patterns. Works well for uniformly growing cities with consistent decade-over-decade improvement.

Exponential Regression

Compounding growth baseline. Fitted using log-linearization to handle very large population values without overflow.

Linear Regression

Simple baseline. Rarely wins, but serves as a sanity check and lower-bound anchor for the ensemble confidence interval.
